Frequently Asked Questions About Free & Open Source Software

Common questions about ditching expensive subscriptions and switching to verified free tools:

Are the software alternatives on Appsolutely Free truly free?

Yes. Every tool is explicitly classified under three transparent tiers: ✨ $0 to use (100% free for personal and commercial use with zero recurring fees), 🎁 Limited free plan (generous free tier with transparent limits), or 🖥️ Requires hosting/hardware (free open-source code for self-hosting). No hidden paywalls or surprise trials.

How much can I save each year by replacing subscriptions?

By replacing common subscriptions like Adobe Creative Cloud ($600+/yr), Microsoft 365 ($70–$100/yr), Spotify ($130/yr), Slack ($100+/yr/user), and Notion ($120/yr), the average user, freelancer, or creator saves between $1,200 and $3,500+ every single year. You can calculate your exact personalized savings using our interactive Annual Savings Calculator above.

Does Appsolutely Free recommend software with adware or bloatware?

Never. We maintain a strict zero-bloatware policy. Programs bundled with unwanted browser toolbars, crypto miners, adware installers, or predatory telemetry are permanently excluded from our directory.

Can open-source alternatives open and export my existing project files?

In most workflows, yes! LibreOffice opens and saves Microsoft Office (.docx, .xlsx, .pptx) files natively. Photopea and GIMP handle Adobe Photoshop (.psd) files. Inkscape handles vector (.svg, .ai) files. Check our Software Switching Guides for step-by-step export workflows and critical gotchas before cancelling subscriptions.

I’m Yishay Stewart-Mitchell, a creator and technology professional from Jamaica, Queens, and the founder of Appsolutely Free.

My background spans IT support, audio production, programming, and custom electronics. Across those fields, I’ve seen how quickly software costs can add up—especially when you’re learning, starting a business, or trying to bring an idea to life.

I created Appsolutely Free to make useful technology easier to find and understand. The goal is simple: help people discover free and open-source alternatives, understand the tradeoffs, and choose tools that fit their needs and budget.

To me, “free” deserves an explanation. A useful recommendation should tell you whether an app has limits, requires paid hosting, or charges for features you might need. That transparency is part of what I want to build here.

Whether you’re a student, artist, developer, small-business owner, or someone tired of another monthly subscription, I want Appsolutely Free to help you keep creating and learning.
